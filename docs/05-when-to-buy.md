# When to buy

There is no universal "cheapest day". There are **distributions**: advance-purchase windows, seasonal demand, day-of-week effects and sale cycles. The reliable move is to know the route's own history and book when the fare is good against it.

All figures below are averages or windows from the cited datasets, not quotes. Air and rail fares are dynamic and route-specific; treat every number as a prior, not a promise.

**Confidence:** **H** = primary operator or agency data; **M** = reputable secondary or caveated primary; **L** = directional only.

### The shape of the curve

The consistent shape is a **shallow U**: high at six-plus months, declining to a trough, then rising steeply inside three weeks.

- **Expedia:** booking 180+ days out is the most expensive bucket — $130 more than the domestic trough and $225 more than the international trough.
- **Hopper:** prices fall on average until 1–2 months out, then can increase by almost double in the last three weeks.
- **Kayak:** Thanksgiving fares bottom around five weeks before and climb sharply within two weeks of departure.
- **Google:** the low-price range is wide (domestic 22–57 days), but the drop across the international range is "insignificant", so waiting inside the range has little upside.
- **Academic:** price dispersion, not just the mean, rises toward departure. Last-minute shopping is a worse lottery.

### Why the sources disagree

The apparent contradiction — Expedia and Kayak saying 8–15 or 7–14 days for international, while Google, Going and Hopper say 2–8 months — is mostly methodology.

| Disagreement | Likely reason |
|---|---|
| Expedia international 8–15 days vs Google 89 days | Expedia averages fares **actually paid** across all international routes, including short-haul Canada/Mexico/Caribbean and late-booked leisure. Google measures the **lowest average price point** across a route mix. Do not use 8–15 days for long-haul. |
| Kayak international 7–14 days | Same short-haul and late-booking mix; Kayak explicitly flags it as a surprise and says "proceed with caution". |
| Domestic 15–30 vs 39 vs 30–90 | Average paid fare vs lowest-price point vs a "safe" window; route mix; whether peak dates are included. |
| Google summer 21 days vs Going "3–5 months for peak" | Google's 21 days is the average lowest-price point for July/August, not a recommendation for a fixed peak date. Peak inventory risk argues for earlier. |
| Hopper 1–2 months vs Going 1–3 months | Hopper monitors price drops; Going builds in availability risk and peak padding. |

**Practical reading:** use the **earliest** end of the credible range when dates are fixed or demand is high; use the **later** end only when you are flexible and tracking the specific route.

## Air: day of week

Two different questions are constantly conflated.

### Cheapest day to fly

| Study | Cheapest to fly | Measured difference |
|---|---|---|
| Google (Oct 2026) | Depart Monday; return Tuesday/Wednesday | Flying Mon + returning Tue/Wed saves ~14% vs weekend; up to 20% domestic vs Sunday |
| Expedia 2026 | Tuesday domestic | Tuesday is 14% below Sunday domestic |
| Hopper 2025 | Wednesday domestic depart; Tuesday return | Midweek flying saves $42 / 14% |
| Kayak 2026 | Domestic: depart Monday, return Wednesday. International: Wednesday both ways | Friday/Saturday most expensive; midweek returns are the real win |
| Going 2026 | Tuesday, Wednesday, Saturday departures | Last-minute deals mostly myth; prices rise in the last three weeks |

**Consensus:** fly **Tuesday, Wednesday or Saturday**; return **Tuesday or Wednesday**. Avoid **Friday and Sunday**. The gain from choosing the right flight day is roughly **10–20%**; the gain from choosing the right booking day is roughly **0–1.5%**.

### Cheapest day to book

| Claim | Evidence (2026) |
|---|---|
| "Book on Tuesday" | No magic day. Google finds Wednesday cheapest by 1.4% vs Sunday — "negligible". Hopper finds Thursday cheapest by about $10. Kayak: "no magic day". Going: the day you buy "barely moves the needle". |
| "Book at midnight" | No documented time-of-day discount |
| "Wait for a sale" | Sales are real but modest and unpredictable; the fare bucket matters more |

The Tuesday myth persists because airlines historically filed fare changes early in the week and OTAs promoted Tuesday sales. That is a supply-side rhythm, not a price law.

### Time of day

Very early morning and late-night/red-eye departures can be cheaper (Kayak, TPG). But morning is not low-demand: Expedia says more than half of domestic and international flyers book morning flights, so it is a high-demand slot. No major study publishes a robust average; route and curfew effects dominate.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| US domestic | **January–February**; late August–October | Sep–Oct | Summer, Thanksgiving, Christmas/NY, spring break | Expedia: January 19% below December. Hopper: Jan/Feb 32% ($100) below Mar/Apr; Sep/Oct 40% ($150) below summer | H |
| Europe | **January–February**; late September–October | Apr–May, Sep–Oct | Jul–Aug, Easter, Christmas/NY | Going: shoulder fares 20–40% below peak | M–H |
| Asia | Jan–Feb outside CNY; Sep–Oct | Sep–Nov, Feb–Mar | Lunar New Year, Golden Week, Diwali, summer | No audited public premium series; directional only | L |

## Air: the sale calendar

| Window | What historically happens | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| **Early January** | Airline January sales (Aer Lingus, easyJet, Ryanair historically), OTA New Year promotions; the UK's Great British Rail Sale ran in the same week | Existence M; savings L |
| Late January–February | Second wave of winter sales, Presidents' Day and spring-break promotions | L |
| Late August–early September | Fall-travel and shoulder-season promotions | L |
| Black Friday (late Nov) | Airline and OTA flash sales, usually for winter/spring travel | Existence M; value L |
| Cyber Monday / Travel Tuesday | OTA and hotel/air promos; Travel Tuesday is largely a US marketing event | Existence M; value L |
| Random midweek | Flash sales, seat sales, mistake fares | L |
| **Data-backed deal months** | **July, October and November** produce the most Expedia Flight Deals (≥20% below predicted) | H |

**How reliable are sales?** Low as a strategy. They are marketing events with blackout dates, limited seats and often mediocre discounts compared with booking the right week. The one high-confidence "deal calendar" is Expedia's finding that July, October and November produce the most genuinely discounted fares.

**Mistake fares:** there is no credible published seasonality. They are operational pricing errors, not calendar events.

## Rail: book when the quota opens

| Country / operator | Cheap quota release | Best booking horizon | Sale calendar | Day-of-week | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UK — National Rail** | Advance tickets typically ~12 weeks (84 days) ahead; some routes further out | **8–12 weeks**; for Christmas, Easter and bank holidays, the day the quota opens | **Great British Rail Sale ran 6–12 Jan 2026** (travel 13 Jan–25 Mar 2026), well over 50% off many Advance/off-peak tickets | Tue/Wed cheapest; Fri/Sun and school holidays peak | H sale; M 12-week rule |
| **Germany — DB** | ~180 days rolling | **3–6 months**; peak dates at opening | Sparpreis and Super Sparpreis are permanent yield-managed fares, not a calendar | Tue–Thu cheaper; Fri/Sun peak | M horizon; L campaigns |
| **France — SNCF** | **4 months** normally; Ouigo Classic 60 days; sales open in blocks (e.g. 12 Nov 2026 for 7 Jan–2 Jul 2027) | **At opening** for peak; 2–4 months otherwise | No single national sale; Ouigo low-cost; occasional flash sales | Tue–Thu cheaper; Fri/Sun peak | H |
| **Italy — Trenitalia** | ~4 months (120 days) for Frecce; regional anytime | **2–4 months**; Super Economy at opening | Super Economy permanent; FrecciaDAYS up to 60% off | Tue–Thu cheaper; Fri/Sun peak | M |
| **Spain — Renfe** | ~3–4 months, route-dependent | **2–4 months**; peak earlier | Renfe promos and Avlo low-cost; no fixed calendar | Tue–Thu cheaper; Fri/Sun peak | L–M |
| **Japan — JR** | **1 month at 10:00 JST**; Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu online reservations up to 1 year | **Exactly at opening** for Golden Week, Obon and New Year; 2–4 weeks otherwise | No sales; early-bird discount tickets at 21/14/7 days | Tue–Thu cheaper; weekends/holidays peak | M–H |
| **India — IRCTC** | **60 days** advance reservation | **At opening**; Tatkal one day before at a premium | No sales; dynamic pricing | Midweek cheaper; Fri/Sun peak | M |
| **China — 12306** | **15 days** presale | **At opening**; Chunyun and Golden Week sell out immediately | No sales; dynamic pricing | Midweek cheaper; Fri/Sun peak | M |

**Release window is not the same as cheapest window.** Booking at opening is the safe play for peak dates. Off-peak, waiting can capture a sale, but the cheapest quota may already be gone.

**The most reliable calendar anchors:** the UK 12-week Advance rule and the Great British Rail Sale, plus SNCF's published opening dates. Everything else is operator-specific; verify at booking.

## Coach: book early, not late

| Region / operator | Booking horizon | Cheapest days | Yield behaviour | Promo cycle | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Europe — FlixBus** | **2–4 weeks** for the cheapest seats; tickets sell up to months ahead | Tue/Wed; avoid Fri/Sun | Dynamic yield: fares rise with load factor; no fixed release | Flash sales and route-launch promos; no reliable calendar | L–M |
| **US — Megabus** | **2–4 weeks**; early booking captures the lowest buckets | Tue/Wed; avoid Fri/Sun | Dynamic yield; $1 lead-in fares are limited promotional inventory | Random route sales and "$1 fare" releases | L |
| **UK — National Express** | Official guidance: **at least 3 days ahead**; cheapest generally 2–4 weeks out | **Monday–Thursday**; avoid peaks | Dynamic yield; limited £7-or-less inventory | "100,000 tickets for £7 or less" promotional inventory; Coachcard discounts | H guidance; M horizon |

National Express is the only one of the three with explicit official guidance: search at least three days ahead, stay flexible, travel Monday–Thursday. That is a floor, not the optimum; 2–4 weeks is the practical cheapest window. FlixBus and Megabus are yield-managed with no published release date.

**Night coach economics:** strongest on 6–12 hour routes where the alternative includes a hotel night. Under 5–6 hours you arrive at 2–4am; over 14 hours, fly or break the journey. Add bag storage, a shower or gym day pass, and the cost of bad sleep.

## How to set alerts properly

Consumer tools are weaker than they look:

- **Google Flights** alerts on every price change, including increases, and has no target-price threshold. Route-anchored; no discovery.
- **Kayak** decides which changes merit an alert; daily bundles plus some faster alerts.
- **Skyscanner** is strong for European LCCs but noisier.
- **Hopper** is app-only and upsells.
- **Airline emails** only see that airline.

The fix is a baseline:

1. Track the exact route, date window and cabin — not just the city pair.
2. Store the daily minimum.
3. Alert on a **new low** or **15–20% below the trailing median**, not on every change.
4. Set review points at **T-90 and T-60** for long-haul and peak trips.
5. Re-price before acting.
6. Ignore "deal" alerts that do not state the total with bags.

See [08-agent-fleet.md](08-agent-fleet.md) for the watchkeeper implementation.

## The decision flow

```
Is the travel date inside the operator's advance window?
  ├─ No  → record the opening date, set a reminder, wait
  └─ Yes → is the fare below the route's trailing p10?
        ├─ No  → set an alert at 15% below the median; keep planning
        └─ Yes → is the itinerary verified and the total acceptable?
              ├─ No  → verify or discard; do not book a lead
              └─ Yes → is it refundable or inside the 24h rule?
                    ├─ No  → decide carefully; this is the commitment
                    └─ Yes → book, then keep watching for a reprice
```

The point of the flow is to stop you from booking a **lead** because it looked cheap in a calendar.
