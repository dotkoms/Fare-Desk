# The playbook

Ordered by expected value, not novelty. The first five cost nothing, carry no risk, and beat every "hack" in this repo.

Do these before anything clever:

1. Search the whole week instead of one date.
2. Add every nearby airport.
3. Check the rail split.
4. Compare a night coach or sleeper against a flight plus hotel.
5. Price the total door to door.

Most travellers stop at the first search box and then look for a trick. The trick is the search.

---

## 1. Search wider

| Tactic | Mechanism | Typical saving | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flexible-date calendar | Fares sit in date-specific buckets; the calendar shows days where the cheap bucket is still open | 5–25% | Cheapest day may be 5am, two stops, or basic economy |
| Nearby and secondary airports | Different catchment pricing, LCC bases and taxes within one metro | 15–25% metro average; up to 60% outliers | Ground transfer, fewer frequencies, no reaccommodation across airports |
| "Anywhere" discovery | Google Flights Explore, Kayak Explore, Azair, Skiplagged destinations | Discovery, not a discount | Deals to places you never wanted to go; prices are indicative |
| Split the party | If one seat is left in the cheap bucket, the whole booking prices up; separate bookings keep one passenger cheap | Small, at bucket edges | Separate PNRs; no party reaccommodation; seats may not be together |
| Compare First Advance | First class is a separate bucket and can undercut a flexible standard fare | Occasionally large | Few seats; usually still more than Standard Advance |

## 2. Restructure the itinerary

This is where the big money is. A different **shape** of trip beats a different date almost every time.

| Tactic | Mechanism | Typical saving | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rail split ticketing | Each segment has its own quota; the through fare is not the sum of the parts. UK Condition 14 explicitly permits it | 20–30% when a split exists; found on ~60% of UK journeys | Separate contracts; a missed connection is usually your problem |
| Air split tickets | Two tickets can undercut a through fare, especially when an LCC competes on one segment | 10–25% net after bags and time | No protection; re-check bags; buffer 4h domestic, 6h or overnight international |
| Positioning leg | Separate cheap flight or ground leg to a hub where the long-haul is cheaper | Only above a $100–200 gap | Two contracts; one 2026 test won 1 of 4 city pairs; buffer 4–6h or overnight |
| Open-jaw | Fly into A, home from B, priced as a round trip; avoids the expensive international one-way | Often large vs two one-ways | You pay for the A→B leg; visa and onward-ticket questions |
| Mixed modes and night travel | Coach, train or sleeper replaces a flight plus a hotel night | 30–50% of trip cost when the hotel goes | Only at roughly 6–12 hours; poor sleep and 5am arrivals have a cost |
| Virtual interlining | An OTA stitches separate tickets into one itinerary and sells protection | Can be large on thin routes | Protection is contractual, not statutory; often paid in credit, not cash |
| Round-the-world / alliance fares | Multi-continent loops priced below separate long-hauls | Niche | Rigid routing and dates; always price a DIY multi-city against it |

### Split ticketing in practice

- **UK:** the deepest market because fares are segment-priced. Condition 14 of the National Rail Conditions of Travel permits it provided the train calls at the split station. Best on CrossCountry, long London–North/Midlands/Scotland routes, and flexible fares.
- **Germany:** DB's through Sparpreis is often already aggressive; cross-border combinations exist but may only be bookable at a Reisezentrum.
- **France, Italy, Spain:** possible; cheap fares are train-specific and non-refundable, so a missed connection is expensive.
- **Japan:** possible because JR fares use coarse distance bands, but not formally recognised; paper tickets and gate-staff discretion.
- **Tools:** TrainSplit, Split My Fare, TrainPal, Railboard, Trainline SplitSave. Company-reported savings, not audited.

### Positioning in practice

- The gap must clear **$100–200 after ground cost and time**. One 2026 test of four city pairs found positioning won only one; the others lost by £12–£188.
- It works between a thin-market airport and a competitive one, not between two well-served airports.
- Book the ground leg before the flight if it is capacity-constrained (ferries, night trains, holiday coaches).

## 3. Buy it better

| Tactic | Mechanism | Typical saving | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book when rail sales open | The cheapest quota is released when the timetable loads; it sells first | 20–50% vs later | Train-specific, usually non-refundable |
| Book coaches early | Coach fares ratchet up with occupancy; the last-week option-value effect only partly offsets it | 20–50% vs the final week | Locks you in; refunds are vouchers, not cash |
| Use the route's own baseline | Google's 2026 averages: ~39 days domestic, ~89 international — but the international curve is flat | 5–15% | Averages hide route variance; "wait for a drop" can backfire |
| 24-hour rule / hold (US) | Direct airline bookings at least 7 days out can be cancelled within 24h or held at the quoted price | A free option | Direct bookings only; the airline chooses hold or cancel; not OTAs |
| Post-booking reprice | Most US airlines dropped change fees; if the fare falls, rebook to the cheaper fare for a credit | Varies | Basic economy usually excluded; credits expire |
| Currency / point of sale | Fares are published per market and converted with stale FX; a foreign storefront can be cheaper | 3.8% of fares show genuine arbitrage; bigger in premium | FX fees, residency restrictions, foreign billing blocks |
| Error-fare protocol | Book direct, get the 13-digit ticket number, screenshot the rules, pay by card, wait 48–72h before booking anything else | 50–80% when honoured | No legal duty to honour; refund is the baseline |
| Stopover programs | Add a multi-day stop at a hub for no extra airfare; some include a hotel | A free city or hotel night | Fare-class and routing restrictions; must be booked correctly |
| Same-day standby / change | Switch to an earlier or later flight within ~24h; free standby on the big three US carriers | Avoids a rebooking | Basic economy excluded; same airports only |
| Last-minute upgrade auction | Bid for a premium seat after ticketing; charged only if accepted | 20–40% of the fare gap | Ineligible fares; inventory is tighter than pre-2020 |

### Error-fare protocol

1. Book **direct with the airline**, not an OTA. OTA holds sit unticketed in a batch queue, which is where airlines cancel.
2. Get a **13-digit ticket number**, not just a 6-digit PNR.
3. Screenshot the fare page, confirmation, fare-rules PDF and receipt.
4. Pay cash on a credit card with purchase or trip-cancellation protection; avoid miles.
5. Use the US 24-hour rule as a free look where eligible.
6. Do not book non-refundable hotels or positioning flights for 48–72 hours. Check PNR status at 48h.
7. Do not call the airline to ask whether the fare is valid; you are alerting fare integrity.
8. If cancelled, request a refund plus documented reliance expenses in writing, then escalate to the DOT.

Honour rates are contested and commercially motivated. Treat the refund as the baseline, the trip as the bonus.

## 4. Rail-specific

| Tactic | Mechanism | Typical saving | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Railcards and discount cards | Operators trade a discount for an annual commitment fee: UK 1/3 off, BahnCard 25/50, Carte Avantage | 25–50% | Time restrictions, minimum fares, break-evens |
| Group save | UK GroupSave and German Länder-Tickets price groups flat | ~33% | Must travel together; standard class; no railcard stacking |
| Border split | Buy a domestic ticket to the last station before the border and another from the first after it, on the same train | 30–50% | Separate contracts; the train must call at the split station |
| Book with the foreign operator | The same international train is often cheaper on the home operator's site: ČD vs DB, ÖBB vs DB, SBB vs DB | 20–50% on the leg | Legal under EU 2021/782; after-sales can be harder |
| Passes, priced honestly | Interrail/Eurail or JR Pass only when many long legs, last-minute travel, or reservation-free countries | Negative on fixed western high-speed trips | Reservation fees of €10–35 per high-speed seat can add €100+ |
| Sleepers | A couchette or mini cabin often undercuts a hotel plus breakfast, and saves a travel day | Hotel night | A peak-price single sleeper can cost more than a four-star room; book at opening |
| Delay compensation | EU rail: 25% at 60–119 min, 50% at 120+. UK Delay Repay: 25–100% depending on operator | 25–100% of the fare | Claim within the deadline; most UK operators require a manual claim |

## 5. Coach-specific

| Tactic | Mechanism | Typical saving | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Night coach | Removes a hotel night and a travel day on a 6–12 hour leg | £70–125 per leg on UK long routes | Bag storage, showers and bad sleep eat the saving; arrival at 5–7am |
| Promotional releases | Operators dump seats in short windows; Megabus still advertises fares from $1 | Large but scarce | Often one seat per departure; ~$4.99 booking fee; route restrictions |
| Student / youth fares | Country-specific verification schemes, e.g. UNiDAYS 20% in the UK, 50% domestic in Czechia | 20–50% | Verification required; usually cannot stack with promos |
| Pre-buy luggage | Extra bags cost less online than from the driver | €5–18 vs more at the bus | Driver can refuse if the hold is full; bulky items need advance booking |
| Terminal choice | Edge terminals have cheaper operating costs and sometimes cheaper fares | Fare saving minus €2–5 metro or €20–40 taxi | 30–60 extra minutes; check the ticket address close to departure |
| Refundable voucher hedge | Cancel 30+ days out for a 100% voucher; 60-minute free-cancel window after booking | Preserves flexibility | Voucher not cash; fees never refunded |

## 6. The gray area

Real, widely used, and carries consequences. Described so you can recognize them, not because a verifier should recommend them by default.

| Tactic | What it is | Why it works | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden-city (skiplagging) | Book A→C via B and leave at B | Fares follow market pricing, not distance | Breaches the contract of carriage. Airlines can cancel the rest of the ticket, refuse bags, bill the difference, void miles. Carry-on only, one-way, no loyalty number, never time-critical |
| Throwaway ticketing | Buy a return and use only the outbound | Return-inclusive fares can beat one-ways | Same contract breach; missing the outbound can cancel the whole booking |
| Fuel dumping | Construct a fare that avoids carrier surcharges (YQ) | Fare construction at agent/GDS level | Not self-serve; violates fare rules; agencies face debit memos and accreditation risk |
| Back-to-back / nested | Overlapping returns to satisfy stay rules | Mostly obsolete as Saturday-stay rules faded | Prohibited in many contracts; segments can be cancelled |

Hidden-city is a civil contract issue, not a crime. The $9.4M Skiplagged verdict was for copyright in booking content, not a ruling that hidden-city is illegal. Germany's Federal Court of Justice held in 2025 that Lufthansa's extra-fee clauses were incompatible with consumer law, while still allowing airlines to refuse further carriage. None of that makes it safe on a trip that matters.

## 7. The only scoring rule you need

**Generalized cost = fares + fees + bags + seat reservations + local transfers + food and lodging + (value of time × door-to-door hours) + risk premium + arrival-time penalty − residual value.**

- **Door-to-door hours** run from leaving your origin to reaching your destination, including access, waiting, in-vehicle, transfer and egress time. Do not use the operator's published duration.
- **Value of time** is your own hourly rate. Leisure travel often uses €10–20/hour; use less for overnight hours you would otherwise be asleep, but not zero.
- **Risk premium** = probability of missing the connection × (rebooking cost + hotel + lost activity + forfeited compensation). For separate tickets, use empirical miss rates, not optimism.
- **Arrival-time penalty** captures a 2–6am arrival: bag storage, shower, early check-in, extra hotel night.
- **Residual value** captures a reusable pass, voucher, or flexible ticket.

Choose a mixed itinerary only if `GC(mixed) − GC(baseline)` is negative. If it saves three hours but costs €90 more, that is €30/hour; if your time is worth €15/hour, the slower option wins.

### Self-connection buffers

| Situation | Buffer |
|---|---|
| Separate tickets, domestic | 2–3 hours |
| Separate tickets, international | 4–6 hours, or overnight |
| Ground-to-ground transfer | 2–3 hours |
| Domestic-to-international | 3 hours |
| Congested hub | 4 hours |
| Anything critical | Overnight before |

With a 30-minute inbound delay, passengers miss European self-connections 40.3% of the time. Carry-on only roughly halves the risk.
